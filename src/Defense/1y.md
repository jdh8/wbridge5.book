# Natural (1y)

A 1-of-a-suit opening usually shows at least average strength and length in
the suit.  Defense against conventional openings is covered in later
chapters, as far as Wbridge5 itself plays them.

Opponents' auction deprives little bidding space and shows strength.
Therefore, Wbridge5 is more preemptive here than in its constructive auctions.

## Systems outside the book

The defensive chapters cover the systems Wbridge5 itself plays: its own,
SEF and SAYC.  A strong 1♣, Multi 2♦, Flannery 2♦, Precision 2♦ and Roman
2♦ belong to none of them and are out of scope.

Wbridge5 has no setting for these openings and gets no alerts, so it reads
them by its own system.  Probing Wbridge5 5.12 shows that it defends
(1♣)-P-(1♦) as two natural bids, cuebidding 2♣ with 5-5 in the majors (246
of 300 hands) and overcalling a natural 2♦ with 6+♦ (162 of 300).  It takes
any (2♦) for a strong, game-forcing 2♦: with 8--18 HCP it passes 201 of 300
hands and never makes a takeout double, and a long diamond suit bids a
natural 3♦, as with ♠J2 ♥54 ♦AK109854 ♣Q10.

## Simple overcalls

Simple overcalls are constructive but somewhat limited.  They show 5+ cards in
the bid suit.  Their strength depends on their level.

| (1y)  | |
|------|-|
| 1♦   | 10+, 5+♦
| 1♥♠  | 8+, 5+#
| 2♣♦♥ | 11+, 5+#

Hands with 16+ **HCP** are too strong for simple overcalls.  They double first
and then bid the suit in the next round.

Probing Wbridge5 5.12 shows that simple overcalls hardly depend on
vulnerability.  Over (1♥) with 6+ HCP, 6 of 300 calls changed at unfavorable
vulnerability and 4 at favorable, all of them jump overcalls, two-suiters or
marginal 2♣ overcalls.  Doubles and 1NT overcalls did not change.

In Wbridge5, (1♣) 2♣ is natural instead of a Michaels cuebid.
However, clubs was indeed an adverse suit, so a stronger hand is required to
make such a turning bid.  This natural cuebid is alertable.

| (1♣) | |
|----|-|
| 2♣ | NAT, opening values, 6+♣

Advances of simple overcalls are in [Advancing overcalls](Advances.md).

## Precise Michaels

Precise Michaels aka **Michaëls précisés** are Michaels cuebids which show
both suits.  These bids are shortcuts to show **5+ cards in 2 suits**.

Precise Michaels are unlimited.  Advancer is unlikely to pass out because the
strain is never a held suit.  Precise Michaels take precedence over takeout
doubles because they are precise in distribution.

To avoid bidding an actual suit, Wbridge5 allocates bidding space as follows.

- **Highest 2 nonadverse suits:** Transfer, the bid below the lower suit.
- **Top & bottom nonadverse suits:** Cuebid or jump cuebid.
- **Lowest 2 nonadverse suits:** Unusual notrump.

### Unusual notrump

Unusual notrump shows the lowest 2 nonadverse suits.  The following notrump
overcalls are unusual.

| (1y)  | |
|------|-|
| 1NT! | UNT by a PH
| 2NT! | UNT

There is no need to express a natural 2NT.  Takeout double takes care of such
strong hands.  In the direct seat, a passed hand cannot hold a strong notrump,
so its 1NT is also unusual.  Probing Wbridge5 5.12 shows that a passed hand's
1NT in the balancing seat is natural, 10--11 HCP; see
[Natural 1NT overcall](1NT-overcall.md).

### Opponents opened a minor

| (1m) | |
|------|-|
| 2♦!  | 8+, 5+♥, 5+♠
| 2NT! | UNT, 5+♥, 5+ other m
| 3m!  | Jump cuebid, 5+♠, 5+ other m

The 2♦ shows the highest 2 nonadverse suits, the unusual notrump the lowest 2,
and the jump cuebid the top & bottom.  Being unexpected, (1♣) 2♦ requires an
alert outside of France, but (1♦) 2♦ is not alertable because this is the
expected meaning of a cuebid.

### Opponents opened a major

| (1M) | |
|------|-|
| 2M!  | Cuebid, 5+♣, 5+OM
| 2NT! | UNT, 5+♦, 5+♣
| 3♣!  | 5+♦, 5+OM
| 3M!  | Jump cuebid, 20+, 5+♣, 5+OM
| 4NT! | Super UNT, 7+ HCP, 6+♦, 6+♣

The cuebid shows the top & bottom nonadverse suits, the unusual notrump the
lowest 2, and 3♣ the highest 2.  The jump cuebid is a cuebid with 20+ points.
Probing Wbridge5 5.12 shows that 4NT depends on shape rather than strength.
A 6-6 hand bids 4NT, but a 6-5 hand bids 2NT at any strength.

### Strength

Most bids above lack description of strength because their strength depends on
seat and vulnerability.

- **Unfavorable:** 13+, but 11+ if passed
- **Both vulnerable:** 12+, but 11+ if passed
- **None vulnerable:** 11+
- **Favorable:** 10+

Probing Wbridge5 5.12 confirms the steps.  Over (1♥) with 5 diamonds, 5+
clubs and 4--13 HCP, Wbridge5 bid 2NT from 8 HCP at none vulnerable (9 more
with 6--7 HCP), from 10 HCP at unfavorable (14 more with 6--9 HCP), and from
7 HCP at favorable (11 more with 5--6 HCP).  For example, ♠9 ♥82 ♦A10764
♣A10752 bids 2NT at none vulnerable but passes at unfavorable.  Both
vulnerable lies in between: 2NT on 188 of the 300 hands, against 219 at none
and 155 at unfavorable.  That example hand still bids 2NT.

Advances and the overcaller's rebids are in [Two-suited overcalls](Michaels.md).

## Natural 1NT overcall

A direct 1NT overcall shows 15--18 HCP, 1NT opening distribution, and a
stopper in the adverse suit.  Advancer plays
[systems on](../Glossary.md#systems-on) as over a 1NT opening.  The balancing
1NTs, the changes made for the adverse suit, and the overcaller's rebids are
in [their own chapter](1NT-overcall.md).

## Takeout double

The takeout double is one of the earliest conventions in bridge.  It shows one
of the two kinds of hands.

- 12+ points with tolerance in all nonadverse suits
- 16+ HCP, unsuitable for a notrump or a Michaels

Because this call usually implies shortness in the adverse suit, advancer only
passes with length in the adverse suit, converting the double into penalty.
Otherwise, advancer is forced to bid a suit as cheap as possible with a weak
hand.  Opener with minimum hand can safely pass to complete the competition
because of tolerance to each nonadverse suit.

Since a notrump and Michaels are far more specific, they take precedence over a
takeout double.  However, a takeout double takes precedence over a simple
overcall because overcall can bid the suit after the double, which makes a more
specific auction.

### Shape of a minimum takeout double

The hand must be in shape to double with < 16 HCP.  Distribution points from
the adverse suit is reconsidered as -1, 1, 3, 5 to upgrade 3-suiters and to
downgrade flat hands.

- 0--3 cards in the adverse suit
- 3--5 cards in each nonadverse major
- 2--5 cards in each nonadverse minor

Probing Wbridge5 5.12 shows that a 5-card major does not rule out the double.
Over (1♦) it doubled ♠KJ83 ♥AQJ32 ♦– ♣Q972 rather than overcall 1♥.

Advances and the doubler's rebids are in [Takeout doubles](Takeout.md), and
advancer's doubles in [Responsive doubles](Responsive.md).

## Weak jump overcalls

A jump in a new suit is always a weak jump overcall by default.  This also
applies to high-level jumps such as (1♣) 4♥.

Weak jump overcalls share the same criteria as [preemptive openings][pre].  For
example, (1♣) 2♥ shows the same length and strength as a 2♥ opening.

[pre]: ../Openings/Preemptive.md

If partner has passed, Wbridge5 preempts as aggressively as with a 3rd-seat
opening, for strength is concentrated in a known opponent.

Unlike a 1st-seat opening, the jump overcall depends on both sides'
vulnerability.  Probing Wbridge5 5.12 over (1♣) with 6--7 hearts and 3--11
HCP, 300 boards per vulnerability, shows:

- **Unfavorable:** 2♥ needs about 6 HCP with 6 cards.  Of the 6-card hands
  with 3--5 HCP, 31 of 38 passed, and most 6 HCP hands overcalled 1♥.  A 7-card
  suit bids a level lower: 2♥ with 4--8 HCP, 3♥ with 7--11, 4♥ only with
  10--11.
- **None vulnerable:** 6-card 2♥ from 3 HCP, but 20 of 38 hands with 3--5 HCP
  passed and many 6--9 HCP hands overcalled 1♥.  With 7 cards, 3♥ with 4--9
  HCP, 4♥ with 7--11.
- **Favorable:** 6-card 2♥ from 3 HCP, and only 3 of 38 hands with 3--5 HCP
  passed.  Most 6--8 HCP hands that overcall 1♥ at none vulnerable jump to 2♥.
- **Both vulnerable:** in between.  6-card 2♥ needs 5 HCP, and 30 of the 38
  hands with 3--5 HCP passed.  A 7-card suit bids 2♥ with 4--8 HCP, 3♥ with
  5--10, 4♥ with 9--11.  Of the 300 calls, 35 differed from none vulnerable
  and 24 from unfavorable.

For example, ♠K83 ♥J109873 ♦K95 ♣6 overcalls (1♣) 1♥ at none vulnerable but
2♥ at favorable.

Advancer responds as to a weak opening; see [Advancing overcalls](Advances.md).

## Gambling 3NT overcall

A *gambling* 3NT over an opening requires a **stopper** in the adverse suit.
This overcall still shows a 7+-card minor led by AKQ with no stopper in the
**other** side suits.

Over a minor opening, 3NT is meant to hold the other minor, so advancer
without enough stoppers to play 3NT corrects to the assumed minor.  Probing
Wbridge5 5.12 shows that it overcalls that minor instead, e.g.
♠72 ♥10 ♦AKQ8642 ♣KJ10 overcalls (1♣) 1♦.

## The 4-level cuebids

The 4-level cuebids are blockbuster takeouts, showing 20+ points and 4+ cards
in every nonadverse suit.  What a cunning way to use 4-level cuebids!

## Over a Bergen raise

By default the opponents play Bergen raises, so (1♥) P (3♣) is an
artificial heart raise.  Probing Wbridge5 5.12 in 4th seat with 8--20 HCP
shows that it passes most hands, even strong ones.  The double shows clubs.

| (1♥) P (3♣!) | Bergen |
|-----|---|
| P   | Up to 20 HCP
| X   | Lead-directing, 8--17 HCP, 5+♣, ♣ stopped
| 3♦  | 10--17 HCP, 6+♦
| 3♠  | 9--19 HCP, 5+♠
| 3NT | 20 HCP (2 boards)
| 4♣  | 13--16 HCP, 6♣ (5 boards)

Opponents playing SAYC have no Bergen, so their 3♣ is natural.  The same
300 hands then drew 224 of the same calls.  The double turns into takeout,
and a cuebid of 3♥ appears.

| (1♥) P (3♣) | Natural |
|-----|---|
| X   | Takeout, 13--20 HCP, usually 0--3♣
| 3♥! | Cuebid, 5♠ 5+♦ (4 boards)

For example, ♠94 ♥Q732 ♦KQ ♣AJ1094 doubles Bergen 3♣ but passes a natural
3♣.  ♠AK ♥AK3 ♦K76 ♣Q10432 passes Bergen 3♣ but doubles a natural 3♣, and
♠AKQ64 ♥Q108 ♦AJ752 ♣— bids 3♠ over Bergen but 3♥ over a natural 3♣.

The 2NT raise does not matter in the same way.  Over (1♥) P (2NT), the
default special 2NT, SAYC's Jacoby 2NT and SEF's 2NT drew the same 300
calls on the same 300 hands.
